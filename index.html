How the physics works

The car weighs 1,900 kg and its motor makes 750 kW. At low speed the motor is limited by torque (30 kN at the wheels); above about 90 km/h it is limited by power, so thrust falls as speed rises. Tyre grip is friction × load, with a little downforce added at speed. Ask for more thrust than the tyres can hold and they spin, which loses force and makes smoke. Air drag grows with the square of speed, so the last 100 m are the hardest to gain time. Turn traction control off to feather the throttle yourself.

Tips for a fast run

Roll onto the throttle quickly, then ease to the green line until the bar can safely rise. Past about 100 km/h you can hold full throttle. The thruster adds 15 kN for 1.5 seconds and can be used once per run.
